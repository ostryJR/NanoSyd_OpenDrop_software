OpenDrop Generator manual

nanoSYD software · source code on GitHub

How to draw droplet sequences for the OpenDrop V4, build whole processes out of steps, questions and loops, and run them, with or without the device.

Getting started

Open index.html in Chrome or Edge. Double-clicking the file is enough: there is nothing to install and it works offline. Safari and Firefox work too, but they download files instead of showing a save dialog, and can't talk to the device.

Your work is kept in the browser automatically and comes back when you open the page again. Still, use save for anything you want to keep: clearing the browser's data removes it.

The screen

Five ideas to know

  1. Frames. A sequence is a list of frames. In each frame some electrodes are on; a droplet moves to the electrode that is on next to it. Frame by frame, droplets move one electrode at a time.
  2. Actions. You don't switch electrodes one by one. You draw actions: a dispense, a path, a mixer, a hold. The app works out the frames from them, so you can move or reshape an action later and everything after it follows.
  3. Droplets have names. Each action belongs to a droplet ("Droplet 1", or a name you give it, like "Sample"). Its short name (D1, Sample) appears on the timeline and in merged names like "D1 + D2".
  4. Time. Every step lasts the frame time (0.5 s to begin with). To pause, add a Wait.
  5. Droplets never touch by accident. Anything that would put two droplets next to each other is refused, with the reason in the bottom bar. Droplets only join where you say so, with a merge.

Your first sequence (two minutes)

  1. Press the square button beside the left top reservoir on the board. A droplet is dispensed in 6 frames, and the timeline shows a Dispense bar.
  2. The board now shows the last frame of the dispense. Press on the droplet's electrode and drag across the array to where it should go. Let go: the path is added, one frame per electrode, as a Path bar after the dispense.
  3. Open the Templates tab and drag Circle mix 2×2 onto the electrode where the path ended. The droplet now mixes there.
  4. Press Space to play it. Press Space again to stop.
  5. Press save (top right, or ⌘S / Ctrl+S) to keep the project, or export to write the OpenDrop .json file for the original controller.

Tip: made a mistake? ⌘Z / Ctrl+Z undoes it, as many times as you like.

Drawing on the board

The tools on the left of the board decide what a drag does:

ToolKeyA drag…

paintPswitches electrodes on or off in this frame, like the original controller
selectVpicks what is drawn and moves it, or reshapes a path

Dispensing

The four buttons beside the reservoirs pull a droplet out of that reservoir (6 frames), starting in the frame on screen. In a process, use a Dispense tile instead (see The tiles).

Paths

  • Drag from the droplet's electrode. Every electrode you pass is the next frame; the path shows in blue with its step count until you let go. Esc cancels, and going back along the path takes steps back.
  • Corner by corner: Shift+click where it starts, then Shift+click each corner. Esc stops.
  • A path starts in the frame on screen. After you add it, the board jumps to its last frame, so the next drag carries on from there.
  • Several droplets at once: go back to the frame where the first path starts (click the timeline's ruler) and draw the second droplet's path. Both move together.
  • Make a droplet wait: put a Wait tile in the process after the step that brings it there, and place it (see Frame times and waits).

Selecting and changing

  • Select by clicking a bar in the timeline, or the action on the board. Its settings appear in Properties: name, droplet, when it starts, and more. Started after other actions, it can then wait some frames first; the time those take shows beside them. Esc lets go.
  • Reshape a path: drag a straight piece sideways, or drag a corner or an end. Double-click the line to add a point; double-click a point to remove it.
  • Move it on the board: ⌥/Alt + drag, or the select tool and drag, or ⌥/Alt + arrow keys. What follows it moves along.
  • Move it in time: drag its bar in the timeline, or [ / ]. While you drag, dashed lines mark where it would start and end; a line turns solid when another bar starts or ends there, so you can see it start right after that one.
  • Reorder the timeline: drag a row's name (on the left of the timeline) up or down. A blue line shows where it goes. The project keeps the order; nothing on the board changes.
  • Turn it: R (Shift+R turns back).
  • Delete it: Delete or Backspace.

An action that carries on from where another one left its droplet is linked to it: it starts when that one ends and takes its droplet name. Make the first one longer and the linked one moves later by itself.

Templates: mix, merge, split

The Templates tab has ready-made operations. Drag one onto the electrode the droplet is on; R turns it while you drag, Esc cancels. Where it doesn't fit it shows red and nothing is added.

  • Mixers: circle mix on 2×2 or 3×3 squares, back and forth, split & merge. After dropping one, Properties changes its size, rounds and direction.
  • Merge: for two droplets one above the other with one electrode between them. Drop it on the upper one (R turns it for side by side). It waits for both and makes one droplet called "D1 + D2" in the middle.
  • Split: one droplet becomes two.
  • Your own: + New gives you an empty board to draw the operation from frame 1; name it and press Save template. Your templates stay in this browser; Export… and Import… move them to another computer.

Paint

With paint (P) you switch single electrodes on or off in the frame on screen, as in the original controller. What you paint is kept as a Pattern. The buttons on the right copy the previous frame, clear this frame, or clear everything (undo brings it back).

Droplets mustn't touch

Two droplets on the same or neighbouring electrodes (diagonals too) would flow together. So the app refuses anything that would make that happen: a template turns red, a path being drawn turns red, a move is taken back, and the bottom bar says why. To join droplets on purpose, use a Merge.

The message names both droplets (D1, D2…), the frame, and the electrodes where they would meet; the other droplet's electrodes in that frame show dark red on the board. That frame may be later than the one on screen, so the other droplet can be somewhere you don't see yet. The usual surprises:

  • A droplet stays where its path ends until something takes it on. A path that ends next to the route of a droplet that comes by later clashes then, even though the lines never cross.
  • Diagonals count: corner to corner is touching.
  • Just left counts too: moving next to the electrode another droplet was on in the frame before.

Timeline and time

The timeline

Every action is a bar from its first to its last frame, on its droplet's row. Curves with arrows show where one droplet's action carries on from another's (as at a merge). A bar with a red outline has a problem: hover it to read what. The red line is the frame on screen; click or drag the numbers along the top to go to another frame. Ctrl/⌘ + mouse wheel zooms; double-click empty space to fit everything again.

With a process, the timeline shows the whole process; see The whole process on the timeline.

Frame times and waits

  • Frame time: how long every step lasts. Change it in Properties with nothing selected (click empty space on the board or press Esc), under Project. There are no separate times for single steps or frames.
  • High voltage: under the frame time, Voltage (50–240 V), Frequency (100–1000 Hz, used with AC) and Mode (AC or DC) belong to the project and are saved in its file. Leave a field empty to keep the device's own setting. A connected OpenDrop with the custom firmware (OpenDropVCustom) gets them when it connects, when you change them, and when you open another project; ordinary frames leave them alone. They can't be changed during a run.
  • A pause: a Wait tile in the process, placed on the board, waits its seconds. It switches nothing on, and the other droplets keep moving meanwhile; when nothing else moves, its frame simply lasts the rest of the wait. Waiting for the heaters works the same way.
  • Older files with times of their own (time per step, a frame's own time, waits on a path) open with those left out, and say so. Exported OpenDrop files from elsewhere with frames of different lengths play every frame at the frame time, with a warning.
  • Step time (instead of Timeline) shows how long each frame lasts (gray: a step, blue: a wait); the bar shows the total time.

Playing

Space or the preview button on the board plays the frames in the browser, each for its time, round and round; ← / → or the mouse wheel step through them. While playing, the mouse wheel changes the speed. With a process, playing goes from step to step through the whole process. To run it once from the start, use ▶ Run (on the OpenDrop) or ▶ Simulate (only in the browser) in the top bar: see Running it.

Heaters and magnets

With the temperature adapter, the OpenDrop has three heaters (T1, T2, T3, 20–120 °C) and two magnets. A setting holds from its frame until it is set again, so temperatures can change during an experiment.

  • Temperature view: choose Temperature above the timeline. Pick T1, T2 or T3, then click in the chart to set that heater from that frame on. Drag a point up or down to change the temperature, sideways to move it. Click a magnet lane at the bottom to switch that magnet on or off from that frame.
  • Waiting for the heaters: select the setting and tick Wait until the heaters get there. During ▶ Run on the OpenDrop, it waits until the readings get there; without a device it waits the estimated time, worked out from the Warms and cools rates (°C per minute) above the chart. The other droplets keep moving meanwhile.
  • On the board: the strip at the top of the board, in the dip of the red frame, shows each heater's target and reading and each magnet. On the standard and magnet layouts, the electrodes over each heater get a coloured outline: T1 over electrodes 25 and 26, T2 over 57 and 58, T3 over 97 and 98.
  • In a process: use the Temperature and Magnet tiles (see example 5).

Processes

What a process is

A sequence plays the same way every time. A real experiment often doesn't: you check something, then decide what to do next, or repeat a step until it is right. A process is the plan of the experiment as a flowchart of tiles joined by arrows:

  • Work tiles (Dispense, Move, Mix, Wait, …) do something on the board. You draw or place their work on the board.
  • Stops (Measure, Ask, Decision, Repeat) are where the run pauses to ask you something, or chooses which way to go.

Press Process in the top bar. The first time, everything you have drawn goes into a tile called Step 1, between Start and End. Board goes back to the electrodes.

Building a process

  • Add a tile: drag it from the palette onto the canvas. Drop it onto an arrow to put it in between two tiles (the arrow lights up: "Drop to put it here"). Clicking a palette tile puts it under the selected tile.
  • Connect two tiles: drag from the dot at the bottom of a tile (its output) onto the next tile. A Decision has two outputs, yes and no; a Repeat has again and done. Dragging into empty space offers a tile to add there.
  • Loops: an arrow that goes back up (for example a decision's no going back to Mix) is drawn in purple round the side. The tiles in the loop get a dashed outline with the condition, like "repeat until ok is yes".
  • Change a tile: click it; its settings are in Properties.
  • Tidy up: Tidy lays the tiles out neatly, Fit shows everything. Drag a box round several tiles to move or delete them together. Ctrl/⌘ + wheel zooms, Space + drag pans.

Problems show as a badge on the tile: an orange ! (for example "not placed yet": its work isn't on the board) or a red one (an arrow that goes nowhere, a value used before it is measured). Click the tile to read them in Properties.

Putting the work on the board

Wait, Message, Temperature and Magnet go on the board by themselves as soon as an arrow leads to them (a Temperature once it sets a heater), after the steps before them; drop one on an arrow between two steps and the step after it waits for it. They have nothing to place.

The other work tiles only describe what should happen; you still say where on the board:

  1. Click the tile, then press Place on the board in Properties. Or double-click the tile (or Open on the board) to go to the board and draw it yourself.
  2. What happens depends on the tile:
    • Dispense is added straight away.
    • Mix, Merge, Split: click on the board where the droplet is (R turns it, Esc cancels).
    • Move: the freehand tool is picked; draw the path from where the droplet is.
    • Step: draw whatever happens in it.
  3. Droplets coming in from the steps before wait where they were left, shown as dashed rings. Draw from them like from any droplet.

The top bar says which step the board is on, for example On the board: Mix · frames 11–23. Whatever you draw goes into that step.

Why are some steps together on the board? Steps with no stop between them run straight after each other, so they share the board: open Dispense and you see Move with it. A step starts afresh after a stop (Ask, Measure, Decision, Repeat), or when an arrow loops back into it. In example 2, Mix has its own part because the run comes back to it each time round the loop.

With a process, the timeline always shows all of it: the steps one after another, in the order a run goes through them, with frame numbers counted from Start.

  • The row of step bands on top names each step over its frames. The step that is on the board is blue; its bars are bright, the others faded.
  • A purple ◆ between two frames marks a stop (Measure, Ask, Decision, a Repeat round); hover it to see which. ■ marks End.
  • To work on another step, click its band or one of its faded bars, or move the red line into its frames: that step opens on the board.
  • preview on the board (Space) plays through all the steps, one after another. It asks nothing and goes on at every decision (the top bar says “Previewing”). ▶ Simulate and ▶ Run are the real run.
  • At a decision, the timeline follows the way that goes on, so a loop that waits for your answer is shown once. A Repeat is shown with all its rounds. The total time above the timeline is for that way through.
  • A step on the other way of a decision isn't on the timeline. When you open it, it is shown on its own, and the top bar says it is "not on the way the timeline follows".

Running it

  1. Press ▶ Run in the top bar to run it on the OpenDrop: connect it first (the button is greyed until then). The device follows the run and is switched off at the end. ▶ Simulate does the same run only in the browser: the device does nothing. Without a process, both play the sequence once, frame 1 to the last.
  2. Each step plays on the board, frame by frame; the running tile glows in the process view.
  3. At a Measure tile you type in the value and press Go on. At an Ask tile you answer Yes / No (or with the keys: 1 or , for yes, 2 or . for no). Stop the run ends it there.
  4. Decisions and Repeats choose the way by themselves.
  5. The run log at the bottom right of the process view lists the values, decisions and times. Click its heading to fold it away.
  6. ⏸ Pause and ■ Stop are in the top bar. Nothing in the project can be changed while a run goes: not the board, the tiles, Properties, the frame time, the layout, opening or adding a file, undo, or a new template. Those fields are greyed out, and so are Send to device and Run on the device in the Device tab; the board's red frame is greyed a bit. You can't step or scroll through frames either (wheel, arrow keys, the timeline or charts): the board follows the run. A second tab of the page is locked too until the run ends. The run goes on when you switch to another window or tab, but not when the computer sleeps: keep it awake for long runs.

A run changes nothing in your project: afterwards the board is back where it was.

Process examples

Each example shows the process on the left and how to build it on the right. Start from an empty project: clear all on the board (undo brings it back), then Process. You get Start → Step 1 → End. If you don't need Step 1, select it, press Delete tile, and drag from Start's dot onto End to connect them again.

1. Dispense, move, mix

The simplest process: no questions, it runs straight through.

Start
Dispenseleft top · Sample
Moveto the mixing spot
Mixcircle 2×2 ×3
End
  1. Drag Dispense from the palette onto the arrow from Start to End: it goes in between. Then drag Move onto the arrow under Dispense, and Mix onto the arrow under Move.
  2. Click Dispense. In Properties choose Reservoir left top and type Droplet Sample. Press Place on the board: the dispense is added.
  3. Click Move and press Place on the board. The board opens with freehand picked: drag from the Sample droplet to where you want to mix it.
  4. Back in Process, click Mix, set Rounds to 3 and press Place on the board. Click the electrode where Move ended.
  5. Press ▶ Run, or go to the board and press Space to play it.

Dispense and Move share the board (nothing stops between them); the timeline shows all three steps one after another.

2. Mix until it looks right

After each mix, the run asks you whether the droplet looks mixed; "no" mixes again.

Start

  1. Build example 1 first.
  2. Drop an Ask tile on the arrow after Mix. Set Question Mixed well?, Answer yes or no and Value ok.
  3. Drop a Decision after Ask. Choose Value ok and Is yes. Properties reads "If ok is yes: the yes arrow, otherwise no".
  4. Drag from the decision's no dot up onto Mix. It becomes a purple loop, and the loop is labelled "repeat until ok is yes".
  5. Drop a Move on the yes arrow and place it: draw the droplet from the mixer to where it goes next.
  6. Press ▶ Run. After each mix a box asks Mixed well?. No mixes again; Yes goes on to the Move.

On the timeline the loop is shown once: Dispense, Move, Mix, a ◆ for the question, then the last Move.

3. Mix until a value is reached

You measure something (for example a concentration) after each mix and go on once it is below 5 mM.

Start
Dispense
Move
Mix
Measureconc in mM
conc < 5 mM
no → back to Mix
yes
End
  1. Build example 1.
  2. Drop a Measure tile after Mix. Set Value conc and Unit mM.
  3. Drop a Decision after it: Value conc, Compared <, With 5.
  4. Connect yes to End (if dropping it on the arrow hasn't already) and drag no back up onto Mix.
  5. Run it. After each mix it asks "Measure conc (mM) and type it in". Type the number and press Go on: 7 mixes again, 3 ends the run.

Comparisons can be <, ≤, >, ≥, = or ≠. To change what the run says when it asks, fill in Ask with, e.g. "Read the absorbance at 450 nm". Ask is only for yes or no; any number comes from a Measure.

4. Wash three times

A fixed number of rounds: no question needed, so use a Repeat tile.

Start
Dispense
Repeat 3×
again
Moveover the wash spot and back
Wait10 s
back to Repeat
done (from Repeat)
End
  1. Make Start → Dispense → Repeat → End. Set the Repeat's Times to 3.
  2. Connect the Repeat's done dot to End.
  3. From its again dot, drag into empty space and add a Move; under it add a Wait with Wait (s) 10.
  4. Drag from the Wait's dot back up onto the Repeat: that closes the loop.
  5. Place the Dispense, then the Move (draw the droplet over the wash spot and back to where it started, so every round starts from the same place), then the Wait.

The timeline shows all three rounds, each after a ◆ "round 1 of 3", "round 2 of 3"… and the total time is for all of them.

End each round where it started. Every round plays the same path, so if the droplet ends somewhere else, the next round's path starts where the droplet isn't.

5. Heat, mix, hold with a magnet

Warm the sample to 60 °C before mixing, then hold magnetic beads with magnet 1 while it rests.

Start

Moveonto heater T1 (25/26)

  1. Make the chain of tiles shown on the left (palette group Heat, magnets, screen for Temperature and Magnet).
  2. Temperature: T1 (°C) 60, leave T2 and T3 empty (they stay as they are), tick Wait until the heaters get there.
  3. Magnet tiles: Magnet 1, Switch it on in the first, off in the second. Wait: 30 s.
  4. Place each tile. Draw the Move onto the electrodes over heater T1 (25 and 26 on the standard layout, outlined on the board once T1 has a target) and drop the Mix there.
  5. Run it on the OpenDrop: the run waits at the Temperature step until T1 reads 60 °C (within the tolerance). Without a device it waits the estimated time instead.

Heater and magnet settings carry on through the following steps, also past questions, until another tile changes them. The Temperature view above the timeline shows them over time.

6. Two droplets at once, then merge

Bring a sample and a reagent to the middle at the same time, merge them and mix.

Start

Dispenseright top

  1. Add a Parallel tile after Start (palette group Lanes); it has two lane dots.
  2. Under lane 1 add a Dispense (left top, droplet Sample) and a Move; under lane 2 a Dispense (right top, droplet Reagent) and a Move.
  3. Add a Merge and connect the end of each lane to it: it has an input for each. Then Merge → Mix → End.

Connecting the OpenDrop

This needs Chrome or Edge on a computer. Close OpenDropController first: only one program can use the USB port.

  1. Plug in the OpenDrop and press Connect… in the Device tab.
  2. In the browser's list, pick the device. It is usually called Feather M0. The dot by the title turns green while it answers, and the Device tab shows the board type (V4.1 or V4.2, with magnets or the temperature adapter).
  3. Press ▶ Run in the top bar: the device plays it, frame by frame, and is switched off at the end (see Running it). Otherwise it sits still: stepping, previewing, drawing and ▶ Simulate stay in the browser.
  • feedback (V4.2): shows where the device senses droplets as dark dots on the board.
  • High voltage in the Device tab: shows the project's voltage, frequency and mode (set in Properties, under the frame time). The device uses them until it is switched off. Save to device writes them to its memory, so it starts with them next time; its sound and the feedback switch are saved with them. Needs the custom firmware (OpenDropVCustom).
  • Confirmed by the device: the nanoSYD firmware tells the page when it has taken the high voltage, saved, or shown a message. If not, the bottom bar says so: not confirmed (no nanoSYD firmware?) means the device runs other firmware, which can't do these (messages are then no longer sent to it, until it reconnects); menu open on the device, try again means someone is in the device's own menu: close it and send again. A message that arrives incomplete is sent again by itself.
  • Temperatures in the Device tab: the three readings and the targets for heaters the sequence doesn't set. Empty or 0 means off, which is how they start; a heater nothing sets stays off. Switch all off, disconnecting and closing the page switch the heaters off too.
  • Reconnect automatically finds the device again when the page opens or it is plugged back in. Switch all off turns every electrode off.
  • A run on the device goes on when you switch to another window, but not when the computer sleeps.
  • Without a device, open index.html#sim and use Simulated OpenDrop in the Device tab to try it (index.html#simstock simulates one with the original firmware).
  • Traffic log (bottom of the Device tab), for debugging: tick Log what is sent and what comes back and every packet to the device and every answer is listed: the time, → sent, ← received or × no answer, what it says in words (electrodes, magnets, feedback, heater targets, screen message, high voltage) and the bytes in hex. Fold repeats turns the status check sent every second into one entry with a count; the temperature readings in the answers don't stop it folding (it shows the latest). Copy or Save… to send it to whoever is looking at the firmware. Only one program can have the port open, so this is how you see the traffic while the page is connected.

Questions and problems

I can't draw on the board.
Read the bottom bar. With a process, the board needs a step open: double-click a work tile in the process view, or click a step on the timeline. While a run goes, nothing can be changed: stop it first.
My path or template turns red and isn't added.
It would make two droplets touch, or it doesn't fit on the array. The message says which droplets, in which frame and where (the other droplet shows dark red); see Droplets mustn't touch. Draw it a step further away, or start it in another frame.
A tile has an orange "!" and says "not placed yet".
Its work isn't on the board yet. Click it and press Place on the board (see Putting the work on the board).
Why are Dispense and Move shown together, but Mix on its own?
Steps with nothing stopping between them share the board. A step that a loop comes back to starts afresh, because the run plays it again on its own (see the box in Putting the work on the board).
The tile says "the droplets are elsewhere".
The step can be reached two ways (for example the first time, and round a loop), and the droplets arrive in different places. End the loop where the droplet started.
Does it keep going when I switch to another window?
Yes: playing, a run and the OpenDrop keep going in the background (the board on screen catches up when you come back). They stop if the computer goes to sleep or the tab is closed, so keep the computer awake for long runs.
The device doesn't appear in the list.
Use Chrome or Edge, close OpenDropController, try another cable (some only charge), and look for Feather M0 in the list.
"Not confirmed (no nanoSYD firmware?)" or "menu open on the device, try again".
The device didn't confirm the high voltage, save or message. The first means it doesn't run the nanoSYD firmware: install it, or do without those. The second: close the menu on the device and press Save to device again (or change the setting).
Where did my work go?
It is kept in this browser and comes back when you reopen the page. In another browser or after clearing browser data it is gone, so save important projects to a file.

Asking an AI for help

When something doesn't work and this manual doesn't answer it, an AI assistant (Claude, ChatGPT, …) can explain what is going on, from a photo of the screen or your own words.

  1. Take a photo or a screenshot of the whole app (board or process view, with the timeline and the bottom bar in it), or write down what you did and what happened.
  2. Attach the file ai-help.md from the app's folder. It tells the AI how the app works: every part of the screen, what each message means, and the usual fixes.
  3. Ask, for example: "This is the OpenDrop Generator. Why can't I draw this path?" or "Why does my Mix not happen at the same time as the other droplet's Move?"
  4. If the AI can't tell from the picture, it may ask for the project file: press save and attach the .odproj file it downloads.

The message in the bottom bar (or the red badge on the board) is the most useful thing to have in the picture. Make sure it can be read.
